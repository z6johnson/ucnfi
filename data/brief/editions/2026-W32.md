## item-4 — OpenAI discloses frontier-model cybersecurity risk escalations — relevant to UC campuses running ChatGPT Enterprise and research systems

### What happened
OpenAI published two cybersecurity disclosures this week: preliminary evaluations of its Astra model showing elevated critical cyber capabilities (2026-08-07), and an account of third-party evaluation incidents involving its models (2026-08-04), both describing new safeguards and tightened security controls being implemented across its platform.

### Why it matters to UC
UCLA deploys ChatGPT Enterprise as its primary campus AI platform — the first California university to do so — and UCSF has transitioned to ChatGPT Enterprise for P3 and P4 data including clinical use. UC's systemwide security policy references IS-3 and prohibits sharing personal information without a fully executed agreement, but the baseline records no process for UC to respond to vendor-disclosed model-level security escalations. If OpenAI's safeguard changes affect API behavior, data handling, or approved-use scope, campuses currently lack a systemwide mechanism to assess whether their existing agreements and data classification approvals remain adequate.

### For the committee
Does the committee need to recommend a standing protocol — sitting with UCOP IT security or the Legal AI Task Force — to evaluate vendor cybersecurity disclosures against campus-level AI deployment agreements in real time?
