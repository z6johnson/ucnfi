## item-2 — UCSB and UCSD launch UC's first B.S. in Artificial Intelligence degrees; no systemwide curriculum framework exists

### What happened
The UC Santa Barbara Academic Senate Divisional Chair's July 2026 newsletter confirms the Santa Barbara Division approved a B.S. in Artificial Intelligence, based in Computer Science, with courses beginning in the coming academic year. UCSD is identified as the only other UC campus offering this degree, making them the first two in the UC system.

### Why it matters to UC
UC has a systemwide AI training program (the UC AI Primer) and a webinar series, but no systemwide framework governing undergraduate AI degree curricula, learning outcomes, or responsible-AI competency standards. Two campuses have now independently launched B.S. AI programs, and the baseline shows no systemwide academic integrity or research-policy instrument that extends to AI degree program design. If additional campuses follow, the system will have multiple independently designed AI degree programs without shared standards on responsible AI education — a governance gap the committee is positioned to surface.

### For the committee
Should the committee recommend that the Academic Senate, through UCEP or a new working group, develop minimum systemwide responsible-AI competency standards for undergraduate AI degree programs before additional campuses launch their own?

## item-4 — OpenAI launches free ChatGPT access for 100,000 academic researchers; UC has no systemwide policy governing researcher uptake of vendor-subsidized AI tools

### What happened
OpenAI announced on 2026-07-29 that it is giving 100,000 academic researchers free access to ChatGPT's most advanced AI models to accelerate scientific research, collaboration, and discovery. The program is designed to lower cost barriers for researchers at universities globally.

### Why it matters to UC
UC has a risk assessment framework that covers procurement, development, and deployment of AI-enabled systems, but the framework's scope explicitly excludes research use. Individual campuses have taken divergent positions: UCSD has IRB guidance on AI in human-subjects research, UCI has IRB guidance, and UCSF has published LLM research guidance — but most UC campuses have no documented AI-in-research policy. A vendor offering free direct-to-researcher access bypasses procurement review entirely, meaning UC's existing P3/P4 data classification controls and BAA requirements may not be triggered. The systemwide OGC AI Alert prohibits sharing personal information with AI tools without a fully executed agreement, but its applicability to a free researcher program is unresolved.

### For the committee
Does the committee want to recommend that the UC AI Council's risk assessment framework be explicitly extended to cover vendor-subsidized research AI tools, or issue interim guidance to researchers clarifying when the OGC AI Alert's data-sharing prohibitions apply to free vendor programs?
