## item-1 — ED rescinds Title VI disparate-impact liability, removing a legal floor that anchors UC's AI fairness review obligations

### What happened
On July 24, 2026, the Department of Education published a final rule eliminating disparate-impact liability from its Title VI regulations, citing alignment with the statute's original public meaning and Executive Order 14281. The rule takes effect immediately upon publication and applies to all ED-regulated institutions, including UC. Source: Federal Register, 2026-15019.

### Why it matters to UC
UC's systemwide applicable-law mapping (ucop_systemwide, policy, has_applicable_law_mapping) explicitly lists Title VI among the legal constraints shaping AI governance, and the UC Legal AI Task Force practice-area chart identifies discrimination and bias as a core coverage area. UC's Responsible AI Principles include a Fairness and Non-Discrimination principle that references civil rights law compliance. With federal disparate-impact liability removed, some of the legal predicate for UC's AI fairness review requirements has shifted; it is now unclear which AI equity-review obligations remain enforceable as external law versus which are UC policy commitments that UC must affirmatively choose to maintain.

### For the committee
Should the UC Legal AI Task Force be directed to issue an updated applicable-law mapping that distinguishes legally required AI fairness reviews from discretionary UC policy commitments, given that the federal disparate-impact floor no longer applies?

## item-2 — OpenAI launches consumer health product connecting personal medical records to ChatGPT, creating enterprise-consumer confusion risk for UC health systems

### What happened
On July 23, 2026, OpenAI announced 'Health in ChatGPT,' allowing eligible U.S. users to connect personal medical records and Apple Health data directly to their individual ChatGPT accounts for personalized health insights. The feature operates under personal-account terms, not enterprise agreements.

### Why it matters to UC
UCSF has an enterprise ChatGPT agreement explicitly approving P3 and P4 data use (uc_san_francisco, infrastructure, has_enterprise_ai_platform), and the OGC AI Alert prohibits sharing personal information with AI tools without a fully executed institutional agreement (ucop_systemwide, security, prohibits_pii_in_ai_tools). The new consumer health product uses OpenAI's same ChatGPT brand and interface, creating a realistic risk that UC clinical staff — or patients — conflate personal-account health features with UC's enterprise agreement protections. UC's current OGC guidance and campus health AI policies were written before consumer-grade medical record integration existed at this vendor.

### For the committee
Does UC's OGC guidance and campus health AI policy language need an explicit consumer-versus-enterprise distinction for OpenAI products, and who is responsible for issuing that clarification across UC health systems?

## item-3 — OpenAI documents novel safety failures in long-horizon AI agents; UC's risk framework explicitly excludes agentic deployments already live on campus

### What happened
On July 20, 2026, OpenAI published an analysis of safety and alignment challenges observed during deployment of long-horizon AI models, identifying failure modes including goal drift and cascading errors in multi-step agent workflows, and describing iterative safeguards applied in response. The report frames agentic AI as a qualitatively different risk category from single-turn model use.

### Why it matters to UC
UC's systemwide Risk Assessment Guide v1.1 scopes explicitly to 'procurement, development, and deployment of AI-enabled systems' in administrative settings and does not address agentic or multi-step agent workflows (ucop_systemwide, policy, has_risk_assessment_framework). At least one UC campus — UC Riverside — is actively building 'The Grove,' an agentic AI assistant on Gemini Enterprise (uc_riverside, infrastructure, has_enterprise_ai_platform). OpenAI's new enterprise agent platform, Presence, announced the same week, is likely to enter UC procurement pipelines. The gap between the Risk Assessment Guide's scope and the agentic capabilities campuses are already deploying is now publicly documented as a safety-relevant distinction by the primary vendor.

### For the committee
Should the committee direct an expedited addendum to the UC AI Risk Assessment Guide v1.1 that covers agentic AI deployments, given that campuses are building such systems without a systemwide evaluation framework for agent-specific risks?
