## item-1 — NIST seeks public input on modernizing the National Vulnerability Database for an AI-shaped cybersecurity landscape

### What happened
On August 12, 2026, NIST published a Request for Information in the Federal Register seeking stakeholder input on modernizing the National Vulnerability Database (NVD) as AI increasingly shapes vulnerability discovery, reporting, and remediation workflows. Responses will inform how the U.S. government's authoritative vulnerability repository evolves to handle AI-generated and AI-detected security findings.

### Why it matters to UC
UC's OGC AI Alert and IS-3 reference policy both treat cybersecurity compliance as a controlling constraint on AI tool use, and the UC Risk Assessment Guide v1.1 flags third-party model security as an elevated risk category. However, UC has no systemwide position on how AI-identified vulnerabilities should be disclosed, tracked, or factored into procurement decisions — a gap the NVD modernization will widen if left unaddressed. NIST's RFI comment window is a concrete, time-bounded opportunity for UC to shape federal standards that will later bind UC's own security posture.

### For the committee
Should UC submit a coordinated response to NIST's NVD modernization RFI, and if so, which campus security officers and national-lab partners (LBNL, LLNL) should co-author it?

## item-2 — OpenAI begins testing ads inside ChatGPT, raising data-use questions for campuses with ChatGPT Enterprise or Edu contracts

### What happened
On August 11, 2026, OpenAI announced it is testing advertisements inside ChatGPT, stating ads will be clearly labeled, will not influence answers, and will include privacy protections and user controls. The program applies to the free consumer tier, but the company's evolving revenue model has downstream implications for enterprise and Edu contract terms and data-handling commitments.

### Why it matters to UC
UCLA deploys ChatGPT Enterprise campus-wide and was the first California university to do so; UCSF has migrated to ChatGPT Enterprise for P3/P4 data. UC's OGC AI Alert prohibits sharing personal information with AI tools without a fully executed agreement covering UC data, and the Risk Assessment Guide v1.1 flags third-party models with bulk data arrangements as elevated risk. OpenAI's shift toward ad-supported revenue — even if initially consumer-only — signals a commercial evolution that could affect data terms in renewal negotiations; UC has no systemwide contract review trigger tied to vendor business-model changes.

### For the committee
Does the committee want to recommend that campuses holding ChatGPT Enterprise or Edu agreements (UCLA, UCSF, UCD) request written confirmation from OpenAI that the advertising program does not alter data-use terms before their next contract renewal?

## item-3 — OpenAI launches Daybreak cybersecurity models on AWS and opens a governed partner program — directly relevant to UC research and lab contracts

### What happened
On August 10, 2026, OpenAI announced that Daybreak cybersecurity capabilities, including GPT-5.6-Cyber, are now available through Amazon Bedrock, and that approved 'Daybreak Red' partners can use these frontier cyber models for authorized vulnerability research, exploit validation, and security testing. The program introduces a new vendor-governed access tier for offensive security AI tools.

### Why it matters to UC
LBNL operates its CBorg AI portal on enterprise contracts with AWS, GCP, and Azure, and its data classification posture explicitly permits non-public data with an approved access plan. LLNL and LANL conduct AI-assisted cybersecurity research under DOE/NNSA directives. UC's Risk Assessment Guide v1.1 does not address offensive security AI tools or the new vendor-governed partner tier — meaning no UC or systemwide framework currently covers whether campuses or national labs may become Daybreak Red partners, and under what review process.

### For the committee
Should UCOP coordinate with LBNL, LLNL, and LANL to determine whether any entity intends to seek Daybreak Red partner status, and establish a minimum review standard before any UC-affiliated entity does so?

UC Davis's AI Council Draft Report explicitly called for systemwide pooled investment in centralized HPC as its first recommendation; this federal award is a concrete mechanism that partially addresses that gap, but without a systemwide coordination plan the benefit may remain localized to UCSD.
