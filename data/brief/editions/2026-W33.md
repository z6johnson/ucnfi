## item-4 — NIST opens public comment on modernizing the National Vulnerability Database for the AI era — UC has no documented NVD-engagement posture

### What happened
NIST published an RFI in the Federal Register on August 12 seeking stakeholder input on modernizing the National Vulnerability Database as AI reshapes the cybersecurity landscape. The comment window is open now and responses will inform how the U.S. government's primary vulnerability management infrastructure is retooled for AI-generated and AI-exploited vulnerabilities. ([6a167f9ca15670f64fc97c3e8076ad25])

### Why it matters to UC
UC's security posture for AI tools is anchored to IS-3 and the OGC AI Alert, and the systemwide Risk Assessment Guide v1.1 flags accuracy, reliability, and safety — including model drift — as assessed risk categories. However, the baseline contains no field indicating a UC or UCOP position on participation in federal AI security standards processes such as NIST NVD modernization. LLNL's AI safety research explicitly references NIST AI RMF alignment, and UCOP's applicable-law mapping includes NIST AI RMF as a governing framework — making UC a materially interested stakeholder whose research and operational systems depend on NVD data quality. The RFI is time-limited and UC currently has no documented channel for responding.

### For the committee
Should UCOP — drawing on expertise from LLNL, LBNL, and campus cybersecurity leads — submit a coordinated UC response to the NIST NVD modernization RFI, and who should own that process?

## item-5 — OpenAI begins testing ads in ChatGPT — UC's ChatGPT Enterprise agreements may not shield all campus users

### What happened
OpenAI announced on August 11 that it is testing advertising inside ChatGPT, with labeled ads, claimed answer independence, and stated privacy protections for users. The rollout begins with free-tier users but signals a structural shift in how OpenAI monetizes its consumer and potentially business products. ([b19ec562c455e753267b0e04107e2217])

### Why it matters to UC
UCLA and UCSF have deployed ChatGPT Enterprise contracts, which UC's OGC AI Alert and the Risk Assessment Guide treat as the approved pathway for data-protected AI use. Enterprise agreements typically exclude advertising and data-for-ad-targeting terms, but UC has no documented systemwide contract review trigger that would surface when a vendor changes its data or monetization model mid-term. The OGC AI Alert requires a fully executed agreement covering UC data before personal information may be shared; if ad-targeting logic were ever to touch enterprise sessions, that agreement posture would be implicated. UC also has no systemwide enterprise AI platform — campuses operate heterogeneous tool stacks — meaning exposure to ad-adjacent data practices varies by campus without central visibility.

### For the committee
Should UCOP's Legal AI Task Force establish a standing vendor-change notification requirement in future AI tool contracts, and should UCLA and UCSF be asked to confirm that their current ChatGPT Enterprise agreements explicitly exclude advertising data practices?
